[Past History]
[Arif Rahman]:
A respected investigative journalist. Known for pursuing stories long after others considered them too dangerous or too strange to publish. Had recently accused someone inside his own newspaper of suppressing evidence. Kept sensitive information in handwritten notebooks. Had become increasingly secretive during the final months of his life.
[Arindom Kumar Biswas]:
Chief Editor.
Had worked with Arif for eleven years.
Publicly defended Arif's journalism while privately rejecting several of his recent investigations. Had final authority over publication and access to the newspaper's archives. Was one of the last people known to speak with Arif before his death.
[Durjo Das]:
Investigative journalist.
Worked closely with Arif but had a complicated professional relationship with him. Had accused Arif of taking credit for their joint investigations. Was aware that Arif kept a private notebook.
Was present at the crime scene that night.
[Jawer Chakrovarty]:
Senior reporter and Arif's former field partner. Had accompanied Arif during several visits to Palashi. Had argued with Arif shortly before the murder. Was known to carry a large knife during field assignments.
Admitted being inside the house on the night of the murder.
[Sultana Rahman]:
Arif's wife.
Had no known involvement in his investigation. Was the only surviving eyewitness to the attack.
Her physical and psychological condition makes her testimony fragmented and difficult to interpret.
[Mahir Hossain]:
Newspaper photographer.
Had documented several scenes connected to the investigation. His photographs later disappeared from the newspaper archive.
[Nayeem Ahmed]:
Newspaper archivist.
Had access to restricted historical material. Frequently assisted Arif with his research. Was one of the few people who could locate old reports without leaving an obvious record.
[Shafiq Rahman]:
Arif's confidential source.
Had repeatedly warned Arif to abandon his investigation. Had knowledge of several details about the case that were never published.
[Kamal Uddin]:
Night security supervisor.
Had access to the building's electrical control panel. Claims he saw one of the intruders before the power was cut. His account has changed several times.
[Investigative Direction]
The first appearance of the crime scene suggests a domestic murder.
Sultana appears to be the obvious culprit.
That theory collapses when her injuries, the staged room and the missing notebook are considered. The investigation then points toward the three colleagues seen inside the house. Jawer becomes the strongest apparent suspect after Sultana identifies him. Durjo's inconsistent testimony creates another possibility.
But the discovery of poison changes the nature of the murder entirely. Someone wanted the knife to tell a convincing story.
Someone wanted Jawer to be remembered as the man who killed Arif. And someone knew enough about Arif's investigation to understand exactly what needed to disappear.
