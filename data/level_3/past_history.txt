[Past History]
[ Arif Rahman ]
Arif's investigation began with a series of disturbing incidents involving dead animals, disappearances, and reports attributed to the Evil Eye's Wrath. He believed the events were connected and kept his findings in a notebook.
His notes reference an enclosed space near the newspaper archive's eastern wing. No surviving entry confirms that he entered it.
A fragment of his handwriting appears in the investigator's casebook, warning that Jawer had already begun something and that the order of remembered events could not be trusted.
Arif's notebook may contain the explanation for the chamber's significance. Its cipher has not been solved.
[ Jawer Chakrovarty ]
Jawer stole the notebook and concealed information about its whereabouts. His connection to the Evil Eye's Wrath is established by the investigation, though the nature of his bargain remains unclear.
The pendant was recovered with the notebook. Jawer acknowledged ownership but refused to explain its markings.
His statements suggest that he knows the eastern chamber exists. They do not establish whether he has entered it or whether he believes it to be physically accessible.
[ Arindom Kumar Biswas ]
Arindom was involved in the events surrounding Arif's death and the suppression of the investigation. His bargain with the Evil Eye's Wrath is established.
His death in custody remains unexplained. The official record calls it suicide, but the circumstances are inconsistent with an ordinary death.
The phrase "YOU HAVE ALREADY FOUND WHAT YOU SEEK" appears in records associated with his death and in the investigator's casebook.
No evidence establishes who first wrote the phrase.
[ Durjo Das ]
Durjo was an early focus of the investigator's suspicion. His initials appear in a visitor register associated with the archive, although he denies making the entry.
He claims to have heard the name Cartographer spoken in an empty room, but insists he had never heard the name before.
His testimony is inconsistent, but no reliable evidence connects him to the missing chamber or establishes that he has seen the notebook's cipher.
[ Mahir Hossain ]
Mahir photographed the newspaper archive at an uncertain date. One surviving plate appears to show a structural irregularity corresponding to the chamber beneath the eastern wing.
The plate's catalogue history is incomplete. Its date and original purpose remain unverified.
Mahir claims to have developed a photograph of a man standing in a room that appeared empty when he returned. He cannot establish who the man was or whether the photograph was genuine.
His testimony is the only surviving account that connects a photographic record to the Cartographer.
[ The Cartographer ]
The Cartographer appears in contradictory records. One entry places his death before the archive's construction; another records a payment to him decades later.
A transcript of an interview with him exists in the investigator's casebook, but no one can confirm that the interview occurred.
He appears to know the investigator's movements, the missing chamber's location, and details of events that have not yet happened.
No reliable evidence establishes whether the Cartographer is a living person, an assumed identity, a collection of identities, or something that cannot be classified by the available records.
The investigator cannot prove that the Cartographer exists.
He also cannot explain how the Cartographer's name appeared in the records before he discovered it.
[ The Archive ]
The newspaper archive's earliest surviving plan includes a chamber beneath the eastern wing. Later plans omit it without documenting any demolition or reconstruction.
The building's exterior dimensions remain consistent across the plans. The missing chamber's space appears to have been incorporated into the existing structure.
A photographic plate appears to show a corresponding irregularity, and the pendant contains mineral residue consistent with the old foundation.
The evidence supports the existence of a concealed architectural space. It does not establish what was kept there, why the plans were altered, or whether the chamber remains accessible.
