[Interrogation]
[ Jawer Chakrovarty ]
Investigator: "You sold your soul to the Evil Eye's Wrath. What did it promise you?"
Jawer: "You still imagine a bargain as two people sitting across a table."
Investigator: "Then what was it?"
Jawer: "A door. A door that opens only after you have forgotten which side you began on."
Investigator: "Did you hide the notebook in the chamber beneath the archive?"
Jawer stared at him.
Jawer: "You have found the room?"
Investigator: "I asked whether you hid it there."
Jawer: "No."
Investigator: "You expect me to believe that?"
Jawer: "I expect you to notice that I answered your question."
The investigator showed him the pendant.
Jawer stopped speaking.
After several minutes, he said:
Jawer: "If you take that thing below the eastern wing, do not bring it back up."
Investigator: "Why?"
Jawer: "Because the person who comes back may not be the person who went down."
He refused to answer further questions.
[ Durjo Das ]
Investigator: "Did you receive an anonymous letter concerning Arif's investigation?"
Durjo: "No."
Investigator: "Did you ever hear the name Cartographer?"
Durjo: "Once. In a room that was supposed to be empty."
Investigator: "Who said it?"
Durjo: "I did."
Investigator: "You said you had never heard the name."
Durjo: "I hadn't. Not until I heard myself say it."
Durjo appeared genuinely distressed. He denied having visited the eastern wing after Arif's death, although an entry in the visitor register bore his initials.
He claimed the initials were not his.
The ink matched the entry above it, which was signed by the investigator.
The investigator had no memory of signing either entry.
[ Mahir Hossain ]
Investigator: "You photographed the archive before the plans were altered?"
Mahir: "I photographed the building. I cannot swear to when every plate was developed."
Investigator: "This image shows an irregularity beneath the eastern wing."
Mahir: "Then that is what the image shows."
Investigator: "Is it an entrance?"
Mahir: "A photograph records what light reaches the plate. It does not record what stood behind the light."
Investigator: "Did you see the Cartographer?"
Mahir paused.
Mahir: "I developed a plate with a man in it. When I went back to take another, the room was empty."
Investigator: "Was the man the Cartographer?"
Mahir: "The first plate showed a man. The second showed an empty room. I cannot tell you which one was wrong."
Mahir would not identify the man in the photograph. He insisted that the original plate had been returned to the archive, but the catalogue contained no record of its return.
He appeared frightened by the questions, though not surprised by them.
Before leaving, he asked whether the investigator had noticed the shadow beneath the eastern wing.
The investigator had not mentioned the shadow.
[ The Cartographer ]
No verified interrogation of the Cartographer exists.
The investigator nevertheless found a transcript of a conversation bearing that title in his casebook. It was written in his handwriting and dated two days before the first anonymous letter.
Investigator: "Who are you?"
The Cartographer: "The person who drew the room."
Investigator: "You are dead."
The Cartographer: "According to whom?"
Investigator: "The archive's records."
The Cartographer: "Then the records are accurate."
Investigator: "You were also paid last week."
The Cartographer: "Then the records are accurate."
Investigator: "Both cannot be true."
The Cartographer: "You keep confusing contradiction with impossibility."
Investigator: "Did you hide the chamber?"
The Cartographer: "You cannot hide a place from someone who has already been there."
Investigator: "Have I been there?"
The Cartographer: "You have the footprints."
The transcript ends there.
The investigator has no recollection of conducting the interview. No witness remembers the Cartographer visiting the archive, and no physical evidence establishes that the conversation took place.
Yet the transcript contains details about the chamber that were not discovered until several days later.
