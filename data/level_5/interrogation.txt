[Interrogation]
[ Mahir Hossain ]
Mahir sits on the floor of the chamber, his back against the wall. The blood moon shines through the narrow window behind him. He no longer looks like the composed photographer who manipulated the investigation. His hands tremble. His eyes move constantly toward the empty chair.
Investigator: The cult has begun. The deaths are spreading beyond your network. You said you gave it a shape. What have you actually awakened?
Mahir: I thought the Eye was an idea. A name that people would fear, a symbol they would recognise, a story they would repeat. I thought that if enough people believed in it, the belief would become power.
Investigator: And now?
Mahir: Now I think I confused the door with the thing behind it.
Investigator: Did you know this would happen?
Mahir: I knew what I intended to happen. That is not the same as knowing what would happen.
Investigator: You sent the anonymous message. You led me to the chamber. You made me connect the evidence.
Mahir: Yes.
Investigator: Why?
Mahir: Because I could not complete the cipher.
Investigator: You orchestrated the entire conspiracy, but you could not finish your own plan?
Mahir: The cult was never the final purpose. It was the instrument. I needed a person who could see the entire structure without belonging to any of its original covenants. Someone who would investigate the deaths, recover the notebook, enter the chamber, and understand what the cult had done.
Investigator: You needed me to solve it.
Mahir: I needed you to become the final witness.
Investigator: You could have told me the truth.
Mahir: Would you have believed me? If I had told you that the only way to stop a conspiracy was to remove every fact that made it possible, would you have helped me? Or would you have arrested me and published the confession?
Mahir looks at the notebook.
Mahir: You needed to discover the truth for yourself. You needed to believe that the answer belonged to you. That was the last part I could not manufacture.
Investigator: The final page is blank.
Mahir: Because there is nothing left to add.
Investigator: Then what do I do?
Mahir closes his eyes.
Mahir: You must stop trying to solve it.
Investigator: That is your answer?
Mahir: It is the only answer I could never make myself accept.
A distant bell rings thirteen times.
Mahir begins to cry.
Mahir: I wanted to be the man who could control what people believed. I wanted every photograph to become evidence, every death to become a message, every fear to become an instrument. I believed that if I could control the story, I could control what came after it.
He looks up at the investigator.
Mahir: But I never understood why the last page was blank.
Investigator: Tell me.
Mahir: Because I kept trying to write myself into it.
Mahir reaches toward the pendant. Before his fingers touch it, the skin of his hand becomes transparent beneath the lantern light. The bones remain visible for a moment, then fade.
He screams and pulls away.
Mahir: It has started.
Investigator: What has?
Mahir: The removal.
The investigator steps back. Mahir's face is becoming difficult to focus on, not because it is disappearing, but because every time the investigator looks away and looks back, a different detail has changed. His eyes are brown, then grey, then black. His mouth moves, but the sound arrives a moment later.
Mahir: Do not let it make you the author.
Investigator: How do I stop that?
Mahir looks at the blank page.
Mahir: Leave nothing for it to remember.
His final words are almost inaudible.
Mahir: And if you hear me again, do not answer.
Mahir disappears.
His clothes fall to the floor without a sound.
The investigator searches the chamber. There is no blood, no body, and no evidence that anyone was sitting against the wall. Only the national ledger remains open, its final page empty.
[ The Final Convergence ]
The investigator places Arif's notebooks beside the ledger. The pendant rests between them. The blank notebook lies open before the empty chair.
Every symbol in the room begins to move.
The marks on the ledger shift into new positions. The lines on the maps detach from their locations. The photographs curl inward, their images withdrawing into the glass. Names disappear from the pages, leaving impressions that fade as the paper returns to its original state.
Outside, the blood flowing through the streets rises from the gutters. It climbs the walls, crosses the windows, and hangs in the air as a thousand suspended droplets. Each droplet contains a tiny reflection of the blood moon.
The howling stops.
The bells fall silent.
For the first time since the cult began, there is no sound from the city.
The investigator opens the blank notebook to its final page.
Nothing appears.
A thought comes, clear and terrible: perhaps the cipher requires a sacrifice. Perhaps a name must be written. Perhaps the investigator must die, or Mahir must be found, or the notebook must be burned. Every possibility arrives with the certainty of a discovered truth.
Then the investigator recognises the trick.
Every proposed solution preserves the structure of the problem. A sacrifice requires a victim. A name requires someone to bear it. A death creates a witness. A burning leaves ashes. Even destroying the notebook would turn the act of destruction into another event that could be remembered, described, and repeated.
The cult had built a world in which every action became evidence of its own importance.
The Hollow Cipher required an action that would not become another part of the story.
The investigator looks at the empty page and finally understands why the notebook cannot be decoded in the ordinary sense. The cipher does not translate the cult's symbols into a hidden command. It removes the relationships that allow the symbols to function. It does not answer the question of what the Eye means. It removes the distinction that makes the Eye mean anything at all.
The investigator takes up the pen.
For a moment, the temptation is overwhelming. One word would make the solution tangible. One sentence would turn the discovery into something that could be recorded. One signature would prove that the investigator had been the person who completed the work.
But Mahir's warning returns.
Do not let it make you the author.
The investigator puts down the pen.
The final key is not a word to be written on the page. It is the refusal to supply the page with another word.
The Hollow Cipher is complete.
The room does not explode. No great light fills the chamber. There is no voice announcing the defeat of the Evil Eye. The lantern simply goes out.
The darkness is absolute.
Then the investigator hears Arif's voice.
“Do you understand now?”
The investigator cannot see anything.
“The Eye needed someone to witness it.”
A second voice answers. Arindom's.
“The covenant needed someone to remember it.”
Then Jawer speaks.
“And the secret needed someone to possess it.”
The investigator wants to answer, but no words come.
Arif speaks once more.
“Then let it have nothing.”
Something moves in the darkness. The investigator feels the presence of countless people standing in the chamber, their faces close, their breaths cold against the skin. There are children, old men, strangers, and the dead. Every person who has ever heard the name of the Evil Eye's Wrath seems to be waiting for the investigator to acknowledge them.
At the centre of the darkness, a single eye opens.
It is not a human eye. It has no iris, no pupil, and no lid. It is a hole through which the investigator can see the same chamber, the same notebook, and the same empty chair. Beyond that chamber is another, and beyond that another, each containing the same objects and the same figure standing before them.
The eye is not looking at the investigator.
It is looking for the moment when the investigator will recognise it.
The investigator closes their own eyes.
The eye remains visible.
The investigator forgets the room.
The room remains.
The investigator forgets the notebook.
The notebook remains.
Then the investigator stops trying to forget.
For one immeasurable instant, there is no difference between remembering and forgetting, between the witness and the witnessed, between the symbol and the thing it represents.
There is no Eye.
There is no cult.
There is no story about the cult.
There is no person who can testify that the cult ever existed.
The world falls silent.
