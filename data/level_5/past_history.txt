[Past History]
[ Arif Rahman ]
Arif Rahman was the journalist whose investigation first exposed the connection between the unexplained incidents and the Evil Eye's Wrath. His death on 5 October 1887 was arranged to conceal the evidence he had gathered. The notebook he pursued contained the structure of the cult's network, but his final discovery went beyond identifying its members. He realised that the cult's power depended on the continuation of its story through witnesses, records, and repeated acts of belief.
His unfinished work became the foundation of the Hollow Cipher. Arif did not leave a conventional key because the final operation could not be completed by adding another piece of information. It required the removal of the relationships that allowed the information to preserve the cult.
[ Arindom Kumar Biswas ]
Arindom participated in the concealment of Arif's investigation and believed that cooperation would protect him. His death in custody followed his attempt to escape the consequences of the covenant he had accepted. His fate demonstrated that concealment could not sever a person from the system they had helped sustain.
His name became part of the cult's history, and his death became another story through which the Eye could be invoked.
[ Jawer Chakrovarty ]
Jawer stole the notebook because he believed that possession of the cult's secret would give him power over its members. Instead, the theft made him part of the chain that preserved the secret. The notebook's apparent emptiness concealed the relationships between the people, places, and events connected to the cult.
The investigator's unexplained statement that Jawer was killed on 14 October 1887 remained unresolved. The final convergence did not produce a body or establish how the statement had been written. It removed the system of meaning that had allowed the claim to function as evidence.
[ Mahir Hossain ]
Mahir Hossain orchestrated the human conspiracy behind the Evil Eye's Wrath. He manipulated evidence, staged incidents, sent the anonymous message, and guided the investigator through the previous cases. He believed that control over the story would give him control over the consequences.
His ambition helped transform a secretive network into a nationwide cult. Yet the events in the hidden chamber revealed the limits of his power. Mahir could manipulate what people saw and how they interpreted it, but he could not fully command the phenomenon his organisation had invoked.
His final disappearance was not independently explained. The investigator found neither his body nor any reliable evidence of his escape.
[ The Evil Eye's Wrath ]
The Evil Eye's Wrath used staged miracles, unexplained deaths, blackmail, and carefully circulated rumours to expand its influence across Bengal. Its leaders encouraged people to interpret fear as proof of supernatural authority and to depend on the cult for protection from the threats it helped create.
The network became self sustaining because its stories survived the individuals who first told them. Every death could be used as a warning. Every witness could become a recruiter. Every attempt to expose the conspiracy could spread its symbols to a wider audience.
