[Evidences]
[ Arif's Final Notebooks ]
The surviving notebooks of Arif Rahman contain his most complete investigation into the Evil Eye's Wrath. Their early pages document the disappearances and unexplained incidents that first drew his attention. Later pages identify the intermediaries, coded communications, and staged events used to expand the cult's influence.
The final notebooks reveal that the cult's power depends on a chain of recognition. An event is witnessed, interpreted, recorded, and repeated. A symbol becomes associated with a death. A death becomes evidence of a curse. The curse becomes a story. The story becomes a reason to obey the people who claim to understand it.
Arif discovered that the chain could survive the death of any individual involved. A witness could be silenced, a document destroyed, or a leader imprisoned, yet the story would continue through those who remembered it. The cult did not require every follower to understand its purpose. It needed only enough people to preserve its meaning.
On the last complete page, Arif wrote:
If the Eye is a story, destroy the story.
The next page contains a correction in different ink.
No. A destroyed story becomes a legend. A legend is a story that has survived its witnesses.
The remaining pages are blank. Their impressions are visible only when placed beneath the notebook recovered from Jawer. Together, the two sets of marks form a sequence that cannot be read as ordinary writing.
A final note appears in the binding, written in Arif's hand:
DO NOT ERASE THE EYE. ERASE THE DISTINCTION BETWEEN THE EYE AND THE WORLD THAT GAVE IT A NAME.
[ The Blood Ledger ]
The national ledger records the cult's expansion across Bengal. Its entries establish that the incidents were coordinated, that Mahir used his photographic work to support the operation, and that local followers were instructed to create events that appeared to confirm the Eye's power.
The ledger also contains a second system of notation. Beside each completed operation is a small mark resembling a closed circle. Beside each event that has not yet occurred is an open one. The investigator initially assumes that the marks distinguish success from failure.
Under closer examination, the distinction is stranger. Some closed circles appear beside events that have not happened. Some open circles appear beside deaths that have already been recorded. A few entries contain both marks, written in the same hand.
The final pages describe a sequence called the Convergence. It requires the simultaneous presence of every name, every symbol, and every witness associated with the cult's origin. The ledger calls this the point at which the network becomes self sustaining.
At the bottom of the final page is a diagram of a circle with no centre.
The instructions beside it are brief:
When every part has found its place, remove the place that makes them parts.
[ The Crossed Eye Pendant ]
The rhombus pendant recovered from Jawer bears the crossed out eye. Its metal contains traces of the same unusual mineral residue found in the mortar beneath the eastern wing of the archive.
The pendant was originally treated as an emblem of membership. The final notebooks reveal a second function. The crossed eye represents a failed attempt to separate observation from consequence. The rhombus represents the boundaries within which the cult's symbols can be recognised and repeated.
The pendant's reverse contains a shallow groove that matches the shape of the empty space in the notebook. When placed against the final page, the groove aligns with the page's impressions, completing a pattern that resembles neither a map nor a written alphabet.
The investigator turns the pendant over.
For an instant, the crossed out eye appears intact.
Then the metal becomes cold, and the symbol vanishes.
[ The Photograph That Has No Negative ]
The unlisted photographic plate from Mahir's archive shows the hidden chamber, the national ledger, and the investigator standing beside the empty chair. It is the same impossible photograph discovered in the previous case, but the image has changed.
The chair is no longer empty.
Arif Rahman sits in it, his face pale, his clothing stained with blood. He is looking directly at the camera. Behind him stands Arindom, his eyes closed. Jawer stands beside the wall, holding the notebook. Mahir is nowhere in the image.
The investigator examines the plate under magnification. There is no visible negative image, no normal layer of photographic emulsion, and no evidence that the image was produced by the apparatus in Mahir's studio.
On the reverse is a sentence that was not present before:
THE PHOTOGRAPHER CAN SHOW YOU WHAT WAS THERE. THE CIPHER CAN REMOVE THE NEED FOR IT TO HAVE BEEN THERE AT ALL.
The plate then begins to darken from its edges inward. The figures disappear one by one. Arif is the last to vanish.
For a fraction of a second, the investigator sees the empty chair.
It has the same shape as the chair in the investigator's own room.
[ The Three Dates ]
The investigator compares the dates recorded in Arif's notebooks, the national ledger, and the blank notebook recovered from Jawer.
The first date is 5 October 1887, the day Arif died. The second belongs to Arindom's death in custody. The third is 14 October 1887, the date on which the investigator's handwriting records the killing of Jawer.
The three dates are connected by the same symbol, but the order of the marks differs in each document. In one, the dates run forward. In another, they run backward. In the third, the dates are arranged in a circle.
The investigator initially believes that the documents describe a sequence of events. The notebooks suggest otherwise. The dates identify three moments at which the cult's history became fixed in the minds of its participants.
Arif knew the truth and tried to expose it.
Arindom knew enough to conceal it and tried to escape its consequences.
Jawer wanted to possess it and became trapped by the secret.
The investigator has done something different. The investigator has connected the events, given them a single explanation, and preserved them in a casebook.
In the margin, someone has written:
THE LAST WITNESS IS NOT THE LAST PERSON TO SEE. IT IS THE LAST PERSON TO MAKE THE EVENTS MEAN THE SAME THING.
The ink is fresh.
[ The Empty Page ]
The final page of the notebook contains no visible writing. It has no impressions, no watermarks, no concealed symbols, and no marks that can be revealed by heat or light.
Every previous page can be connected to a name, a date, a location, or an event. The final page has no such connection.
The investigator compares it with the other pages and discovers that its edges are perfectly aligned, although the notebook has been damaged and distorted by moisture. The paper contains no fibres from the surrounding pages. Under the lens, its surface appears to recede beyond the physical depth of the sheet.
The page is not a missing piece of the cipher.
It is the only part that has never been written.
Arif's final note refers to it as the hollow. The national ledger calls it the point of convergence. Mahir's private register calls it the reset.
The investigator places the page beneath the blood moon's light.
Nothing happens.
Then every sound in the chamber stops.
The lantern flame freezes. The dust hangs in the air. The blood outside the window becomes motionless. Even the investigator's breathing seems to have ceased.
For one impossible moment, the world has no next moment.
When movement returns, the page remains blank.
A sentence appears in the investigator's casebook, not on the page itself:
THE CIPHER DOES NOT TELL YOU WHAT TO WRITE. IT TELLS YOU WHAT MUST NO LONGER BE WRITTEN.
[ The Last Instruction ]
A hidden compartment opens in the underside of the chamber table when the pendant is placed beside the empty page. Inside lies a small sheet of paper bearing a final note in Arif's handwriting.
The note is not a translation key. It contains no alphabet, no substitution table, and no instructions for decoding symbols. It is a warning about the nature of the final act.
The cult has made a world in which every death proves the Eye, every witness confirms the story, and every attempt to expose it gives it another name.
You cannot destroy it by killing the people who believe in it. Their deaths will become its scripture.
You cannot destroy it by revealing the truth. The truth will become its testimony.
You cannot destroy it by making the world forget. Forgetting leaves a space where the story can return.
The only escape is a state in which there is no distinction between the story and its absence. No witness to confirm it. No record to preserve it. No name to summon it.
The Hollow Cipher is not the answer to the Eye.
It is the answer to the question that allows the Eye to exist.
The final line is written so faintly that the investigator must hold the paper against the lantern to read it.
If you complete it, do not expect to remember why you did.
