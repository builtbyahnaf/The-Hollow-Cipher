[Time] 2nd December, 1887 | 11:47 PM
[Location] The Palassy Grounds, Dhaka
[Statement]
At midnight, Dhaka began to bleed.
It started in the old quarter, where a watchman fell dead beside a locked gate, his mouth filled with black water. Before anyone could summon a physician, three more bodies were discovered in neighbouring streets. A woman collapsed while praying before an unlit shrine. A magistrate was found sitting upright at his dining table, every mirror in his house turned toward the wall. At the riverbank, a boat drifted ashore carrying seven passengers who had drowned without a drop of water in their lungs.
Then the bells began to ring.
Not from a single temple, mosque, church, or clock tower, but from everywhere at once. Some bells had no ropes. Others hung in buildings that had been abandoned for decades. The great clock above the newspaper district struck thirteen, stopped, and began counting backward.
The full moon rose above Dhaka, swollen and red as an open wound. Its light washed over rooftops, flooded narrow alleys, and turned every puddle into a black mirror. People crowded into their homes and barred their doors. Those who looked through their windows saw figures standing in the streets, perfectly still, their faces turned toward the moon. Those who looked again found the streets empty.
Then the screaming began.
Blood seeped between the stones of the roads. It gathered in the gutters and flowed downhill, although the streets were dry and the dead had no visible wounds. It passed beneath doors, climbed the steps of locked houses, and gathered around the foundations of the old newspaper archive. Dogs fled the city. Horses tore free of their harnesses. Somewhere beyond the rooftops, wolves howled, although no one in Dhaka could remember the last time a wolf had been seen within a hundred miles.
And from every corner of the city came the same whisper.
The Eye has opened.
The investigator stood at the archive window, watching the blood flow through the streets, and understood that Mahir Hossain had not been bluffing. The Evil Eye's Wrath had begun its final operation. The network that had once relied on rumours, staged miracles, and carefully arranged deaths had become something no longer confined to its members. Its symbols appeared on doors whose owners had never heard of the cult. Strangers spoke the same words in languages they had never learned. Men who had never met Arif Rahman awoke with his name written beneath their fingernails.
Across Bengal, reports arrived faster than they could be read. Deaths without injuries. Children describing rooms they had never entered. Families hearing familiar voices from sealed wells. Entire households found kneeling before blank walls. The cult had become a nationwide phenomenon, and its members were no longer the only people participating in it.
The investigator returned to the hidden chamber beneath the eastern wing. Mahir was gone. The national ledger lay open, its pages fluttering although the air was still. Every name had been crossed out except one: the investigator's.
Beneath it was a final instruction.
WHEN THE LAST WITNESS REMEMBERS, THE FIRST SIN WILL BE COMPLETE.
The notebook recovered from Jawer remained on the table. Its pages had always appeared blank, but under the blood moon, the impressions within them had become visible. Lines connected the deaths, the photographs, the anonymous message, the curses, and the impossible records. The notebook did not contain a confession. It contained the architecture of the entire nightmare.
Arif had discovered the first layer. Jawer had stolen the second. Mahir had concealed the third. The investigator had assembled the rest.
Yet the final page contained no name, no location, and no instruction that could be followed.
It contained an empty space.
The investigator placed the pendant beside it. The crossed out eye seemed to disappear into the darkness of the page. For the first time, the notebook's title became visible beneath the impressions, as though the words had been waiting for the light of the blood moon.
THE HOLLOW CIPHER.
Arif's surviving notebooks had been analysed by the investigator and the remaining members of the newspaper staff. The final clues had emerged only after a desperate search through his notes, during which a collapsing passage nearly buried the investigator alive. The surviving pages revealed how the cult's symbols connected its local cells, how its members used staged incidents to create belief, and how the curses attached to Arif, Arindom, and Jawer had followed their attempts to conceal, escape, or possess the secret.
But the notebooks stopped just before the final answer.
They revealed the mechanism by which the cult spread. They exposed the people who had carried out its work. They explained how Mahir had manipulated the investigation and used truth itself as a means of control.
They did not reveal how to stop it.
Every page pointed toward the same conclusion, then withdrew from it. The symbols could be translated, but the translation produced another set of symbols. The dates could be ordered, but every possible sequence led back to the night Arif died. The names could be removed from the ledger, but they reappeared in the notebook's impressions.
The cult could not be defeated by killing its leaders. Mahir had already disappeared. Its local cells had begun operating independently. Arresting its followers would only create more martyrs. Destroying the documents would leave the stories intact. Exposing the truth would spread the symbols to people who had never encountered them.
There was only one thing left to find.
The final key.
At three minutes before midnight, the archive's doors slammed shut. Every lantern extinguished at once. The investigator heard footsteps moving through the corridors, accompanied by the sound of a camera being wound.
A voice spoke from the darkness.
“You have spent five cases learning what happened.”
A shutter clicked.
“Now you must decide whether it should have happened at all.”
The lanterns returned.
Mahir stood at the opposite end of the chamber, holding no camera. His clothes were soaked in blood, yet there was no wound on his body. Behind him, the wall was covered with photographs of the investigator at every stage of the investigation.
Some showed the investigator entering rooms that had not yet been discovered. Others showed the investigator reading documents that had not yet been written. One showed the investigator standing beside Arif's body on 5 October 1887.
The investigator had never seen that scene.
Mahir raised his empty hands.
“The cult is already beyond my command,” he said. “I wanted to make the nation believe in the Eye. I never promised that I could control what answered.”
Outside, something enormous struck the archive's foundations.
Dust fell from the ceiling. The blood moon filled the window.
Mahir looked at the notebook.
“You have the names. You have the history. You have the truth. But the truth is precisely what keeps it alive.”
The investigator looked down at the empty space on the final page.
For the first time, the meaning of the title became clear.
The cipher was not hiding a word.
It was hiding the absence of one.

What is it?
