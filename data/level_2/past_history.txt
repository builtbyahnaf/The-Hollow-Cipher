[Past History]
[ Arif Rahman ]
Arif was an investigative journalist whose final investigation concerned a series of mutilations, disappearances, and unexplained deaths across Dhaka.
He called the phenomenon Evil Eye's Wrath.
His investigation gradually led him toward a network of individuals who appeared to share information, suppress records, and manipulate public accounts of certain events.
Arif believed the network was organized. He also suspected that some of its members possessed knowledge that could not be explained by ordinary means. His notebook contained his observations, names, private correspondence, and a collection of symbols he had been unable to interpret.
Before his death, Arif became increasingly secretive. He stopped discussing his findings with his colleagues and began keeping separate copies of his notes.
The notebook disappeared during the murder investigation.
Its contents have never been fully recovered.
[ Arindom Kumar Biswas ]
Arindom had been chief editor for eleven years. He controlled the newspaper's publication schedule, restricted access to sensitive records, and maintained relationships with officials who could influence which stories reached the public.
He had privately rejected several of Arif's investigations.
After Arif's murder, Arindom was arrested on suspicion of involvement in the killing and the disappearance of the notebook.
His arrest appeared to close the investigation's first major thread.
His death reopened it.
Arindom's final statement suggests that he knew something about the notebook's whereabouts. The inscription in his cell suggests that someone wanted the investigator to believe the notebook had already been found.
Neither conclusion explains how he died.
 [ Durjo Das ]
Durjo was an investigative journalist who worked closely with Arif. Their professional relationship was marked by rivalry, resentment, and a shared interest in stories that others considered dangerous.
Durjo knew about the notebook. He had access to the newspaper archive and had been present during the original investigation. He had also concealed information from the police. His actions make him a credible suspect in the disappearance of the notebook, but no reliable account places him inside Arindom's cell on the night of the death.
Durjo's greatest weakness is not his guilt.
It is that he understands how guilty he looks.
And he knows the investigator understands it too.
[ Jawer Chakrovarty ]
Jawer was Arif's former field partner and a senior reporter at the newspaper.
He had accompanied Arif to several locations associated with Evil Eye's Wrath. He was familiar with the people Arif investigated, the documents he collected, and the private methods he used to verify information.
Jawer was present at Arif's house on the night of the murder. He was also one of the people capable of accessing the newspaper's archives without drawing attention.
He is not known to have a history of mental illness, and no reliable witness has described him behaving irrationally.
That may be precisely what makes him dangerous. 
[ Mahir Hossain ]
Mahir documented several scenes associated with Arif's investigation. His photographs disappeared from the newspaper archive shortly before Arif's death.
He has an excellent visual memory but increasingly doubts his recollections.
He claims that some photographs contain details he cannot remember capturing. He also reports occasional discrepancies between what he sees through the camera lens and what appears in the developed image.
His testimony may reveal important information.
It may also be unreliable.
[ Nayeem Ahmed ]
Nayeem managed the newspaper's physical archive and knew where its most sensitive documents were stored.
He had helped Arif locate old reports connected to the investigation. He denies creating the catalogue entry for The Hollow Register. His knowledge of the archive makes him an important witness in the notebook's disappearance.
His fear of the records suggests that he knows more than he admits.
[ Shafiq Rahman ]
Shafiq supplied Arif with information about unexplained deaths and missing persons.
He repeatedly warned Arif to abandon his investigation.
His statement that the notebook was a record of future events remains unverified. Nevertheless, his knowledge of details not yet made public suggests that he may have access to information unavailable to the other suspects.
His subsequent arrest has made him difficult to question.
[ Kamal Uddin ]
Kamal worked in night security and had access to the electrical systems of several buildings associated with the investigation.
His accounts of the night Arindom died have changed repeatedly.
He now claims to remember events that contradict his earlier testimony.
The contradictions are too extensive to dismiss as ordinary forgetfulness, but they do not establish whether he is lying, traumatized, or experiencing something genuinely abnormal.
[ Evil Eye's Wrath ]
At the beginning of Level 1, Evil Eye's Wrath remains an unresolved entity.
Arif believed it was responsible for a series of connected incidents. The evidence suggests that at least some of its members had access to confidential records, manipulated witnesses, and deliberately concealed information.
Jawer appears to have a connection to a subgroup within the organization.
The subgroup's precise purpose is not yet known. Its methods seem to involve more than ordinary intimidation: witnesses report altered memories, impossible coincidences, and experiences that appear to violate the physical limits of their surroundings.
The organization does not rely solely on secrecy.
It relies on making the truth impossible to distinguish from a lie.
