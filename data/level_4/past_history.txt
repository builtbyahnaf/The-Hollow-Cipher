[Past History]
[ Arif Rahman ]
Arif was a journalist who investigated the disappearances and unnatural incidents attributed to the Evil Eye's Wrath. He uncovered evidence that many of the incidents were manufactured to encourage fear and recruit followers. His investigation also brought him into contact with records that appeared to describe events before they happened.
Arif died on 5 October 1887. His death was presented as a violent attack, but poison was the actual cause. The knife wound was part of the scene arranged around his death. The notebook he sought contained evidence connecting the newspaper archive to the cult's expanding network.
[ Arindom Kumar Biswas ]
Arindom was the newspaper's chief editor and a participant in the cover up surrounding Arif's investigation. He believed his cooperation would protect his position and spare him from the cult's retaliation. His death in custody, with his eyes torn out and his body arranged in a posture resembling prayer, followed his attempt to escape the obligations he had accepted.
The chamber ledger describes his fate as a correction. It does not explain whether the mutilation was carried out by a person, a ritual, or something the cult itself did not fully understand.
[ Jawer Chakrovarty ]
Jawer stole the notebook and concealed his involvement with a subgroup of the Evil Eye's Wrath. He believed that possessing the notebook would give him power over the people whose names it contained. Instead, the theft made him a custodian of evidence he could neither understand nor safely surrender.
The investigator's casebook contains a statement claiming that Jawer was killed on 14 October 1887. No body or independent witness confirms the event. The date is also the date attached to the unfinished photograph and several entries in the chamber ledger.
[ Mahir Hossain ]
Mahir was the photographer associated with the newspaper's investigations. His work gave him access to the subjects, locations, and private records that later appeared in the cult's archive. The recovered plates and ledger establish that he coordinated the staging of incidents, the distribution of evidence, and the recruitment methods used by the Evil Eye's Wrath.
Mahir understood the power of an image. A photograph could establish that a person had been present, suggest that a meeting had occurred, or create the appearance of an event that no witness remembered. He used that trust to make the cult's influence appear larger, older, and more inevitable than any ordinary organisation could have been.
The evidence identifies him as the human architect of the conspiracy. It does not establish that he controlled every event associated with it.
[ The Evil Eye's Wrath ]
The Evil Eye's Wrath sought to establish a nationwide cult by turning fear into authority. Its organisers planned incidents that would appear supernatural, circulated stories that gave those incidents meaning, and offered protection to those frightened by the results. Local cells would appear independent while sharing symbols, instructions, and methods.
Arif's investigation threatened to expose the operation. Arindom's complicity helped conceal it. Jawer's theft complicated the chain of evidence and tied him more deeply to the group. Their fates were recorded as consequences of the same covenant, although the full nature of that covenant remains uncertain.
Some evidence points to deliberate human manipulation. Other evidence resists any explanation based solely on deception. The cult may have exploited belief in a supernatural power, but the chamber records suggest that its members encountered something they could not entirely command.
