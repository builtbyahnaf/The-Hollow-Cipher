[Interrogation]
[ Durjo Das ]
Durjo appears exhausted. He has spent the night reading the recovered ledger, and the pages have left black stains across his fingers.
Investigator: You knew Arif was close to exposing the cult.
Durjo: I knew he had found a pattern. I did not know the pattern had found him first.
Investigator: Did Mahir know what Arif was investigating?
Durjo: Mahir photographed the evidence before Arif finished collecting it. Once, Arif showed me a photograph of a document he had not yet written. He thought someone had forged it to frighten him.
Investigator: And you?
Durjo: I told him to burn it. He asked me how you burn a thing that has not happened yet.
Durjo looks at the notebook.
Durjo: Arif began to believe the investigation was being written around him. Every time he changed direction, another clue appeared. Every time he rejected a theory, a new piece of evidence made it seem possible again. He thought he was outsmarting someone. Then he began to suspect that his mistakes were the only things the other person needed.
Investigator: Why did you not tell me this before?
Durjo: Because I was afraid you would recognise the feeling.
[ Mahir Hossain ]
Mahir enters without a camera. His hands are clean, his cuffs immaculate. He looks at the ledger, the photographs, and the notebook without asking how they were recovered.
Investigator: The plates place you at the scenes involving Arif, Arindom, and Jawer. The ledger connects you to the cult's national operation. The anonymous letter came from your studio.
Mahir: Then you have found a great deal.
Investigator: You sent the letter.
Mahir: Yes.
Investigator: You directed the investigation.
Mahir: I arranged its light.
Investigator: You orchestrated the Evil Eye's Wrath.
Mahir: I gave men who were already hungry a table at which to eat. Some wanted wealth. Some wanted revenge. Some wanted to be feared. Some wanted to believe that their cruelty served something greater than themselves. I gave each of them a story that made their desires feel inevitable.
Investigator: You staged the miracles.
Mahir: Some of them.
Investigator: And the rest?
Mahir looks toward the sealed wall.
Mahir: You have seen enough to know that not every photograph can be explained by the person holding the camera.
Investigator: Arif died because he found your operation.
Mahir: Arif died because he believed truth would make him untouchable. He found the mechanism, but he mistook understanding it for escaping it.
Investigator: Arindom helped you.
Mahir: Arindom helped himself. He thought the Eye was a weapon he could put down when the room became dangerous. When he tried to withdraw, he discovered that the weapon had already learned his name.
Investigator: And Jawer?
Mahir's expression changes. It is the first time he appears genuinely interested.
Mahir: Jawer wanted the notebook. He thought it contained a secret he could steal. He never understood that the secret was not inside the book. It was in the person who believed the book belonged to them.
Investigator: Did you kill Jawer?
Mahir: You have written that you did.
Investigator: I did not write it.
Mahir: Then you have a more serious problem than murder.
Mahir leans forward.
Mahir: Tell me, investigator. When you found the first clue, did you feel relief? When you found the second, did you feel clever? When the evidence contradicted you, did you abandon your theory or search for a more complicated one that would preserve it?
Investigator: What are you suggesting?
Mahir: That a person can be led by the nose without ever being forced to move. That a prisoner can mistake the walls of their cell for the boundaries of the world. That a man can follow a trail of truths and arrive at a lie he would defend with his life.
Investigator: You made me do this.
Mahir: No. I made certain you would want to.
Mahir glances at the blank notebook.
Mahir: The worst thing I ever learned about people is that they do not need to be deceived about everything. Give them enough truth, and they will defend the lie themselves.
Investigator: Why me?
Mahir does not answer immediately. He looks past the investigator, toward a point just above the left shoulder.
Mahir: Because you kept asking who had done it. You never asked why the evidence wanted you to know.
Investigator: What does that mean?
Mahir: Look at the photographs again. Not at the faces. At the shadows.
The investigator turns toward the table.
When they look back, Mahir is still seated, but his eyes are closed. The guard outside the room insists that no one has entered or left. Mahir opens his eyes and asks why the investigator has been standing alone in the room for so long.
He denies that the conversation has occurred.
The transcript is already in the investigator's casebook.
[ The Chamber After the Confession ]
The investigator returns to the hidden chamber after midnight. The ledger remains open to the page containing the investigator's name. The final instruction has changed.
The original words, Do not recruit. Let the investigation do it, have been crossed out.
Beneath them is written:
THE INVESTIGATOR HAS ARRIVED. THE RECRUITMENT IS COMPLETE.
The investigator searches the chamber for a hidden writer, a concealed mechanism, or a second passage. There is nothing. The walls are solid. The door is locked from the inside. The lantern burns steadily.
Then the investigator notices that the photographs on the wall have changed.
Arif's portrait is no longer taken from the side. He is looking directly at the camera. Arindom's eyes are no longer open. Jawer's hands are empty. The photograph of Mahir shows him standing behind the investigator, but the figure in the foreground is now facing the wall.
The investigator removes the photograph and turns it over.
A new sentence has appeared beneath the old one:
YOU ARE LOOKING AT THE WRONG SIDE.
The investigator turns the photograph toward the light.
For an instant, the image shows a different room: the newspaper office, years earlier. Arif is seated at his desk. Mahir stands beside him, holding a camera. Between them sits a young figure whose face has been scratched away.
The investigator recognises the room.
They recognise the desk.
They recognise the handwriting on the papers.
They cannot explain why the figure without a face is wearing their own clothes.
A knock sounds from behind the wall.
Then another.
Then a third.
The investigator presses an ear to the bricks.
A voice from the other side whispers:
“You have found him.”
The investigator steps back.
The voice continues.
“Now tell me who found you.”
